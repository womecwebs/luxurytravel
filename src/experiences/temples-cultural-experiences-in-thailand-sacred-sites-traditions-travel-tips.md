faqs:
  - question: Do I need to follow a dress code when visiting temples in Thailand?
    answer: Yes, visitors must wear modest clothing that covers shoulders and knees.
      Short shorts, sleeveless tops, and revealing clothing are not allowed
      inside temple grounds. Many temples offer cover-ups for visitors who are
      not properly dressed.
  - question: Are Thailand’s temples free to enter?
    answer: Some temples are free, while major temples and historical sites may
      charge a small entrance fee. Fees help with maintenance and preservation.
      Prices are usually affordable and clearly displayed at entrances.
  - question: Can non-Buddhists visit and enter temples?
    answer: Yes, non-Buddhists are welcome to visit temples in Thailand as long as
      they follow local etiquette and behave respectfully. Temples are open to
      visitors from all backgrounds.
  - question: Is it better to visit temples with a guide?
    answer: Guided tours are recommended for travelers who want historical and
      cultural context. Guides explain symbolism, rituals, and history that may
      not be obvious during self-guided visits.
  - question: How much time should I plan for temple and cultural visits?
    answer: Most travelers spend 1 to 3 hours visiting major temples or historical
      sites. Cultural-focused destinations like Ayutthaya or Chiang Mai may
      require a full day to explore properly.

Temples and cultural experiences are at the heart of Thailand’s identity. The country is home to thousands of Buddhist temples, known as *wats*, alongside royal palaces, historical ruins, and living traditions practiced daily. Visiting temples in Thailand is not only a sightseeing activity but a cultural experience that offers insight into religion, history, and Thai values.
